Privacy Act of 1974 · System of Records Notices

How quiet changes to federal privacy notices opened the door to data misuse

The Privacy Act requires federal agencies to publish a System of Records Notice, or SORN, for each database of personal information they keep. The notice says what the agency collects, why, and who it can share it with. Since 2025, agencies have rewritten several of these notices to allow new uses of people's data, and some of those uses have since been reported in the news. The basics

What is a SORN?

  1. 01

    Agencies must publish a notice

    Under the Privacy Act of 1974, every federal database of personal records needs a System of Records Notice in the Federal Register.

  2. 02

    The notice sets limits

    It describes who is in the system, what information is collected and for what purpose, and its “routine uses,” meaning the outside parties that can receive records without the person's consent.

  3. 03

    Agencies can change those limits

    To expand a system, an agency publishes a revised notice. New routine uses require 30 days' notice and a chance to comment, but few people outside government read these notices.

About this project

All quoted SORN text comes directly from the Federal Register, and each case links to the original notices. We compare each notice with the version before it. Where an agency created a new system, we compare it with the closest earlier notice and note that on the page.

Highlighted text was added, struck-through text was removed, and orange flags mark language we connect to a reported harm. The “Why this matters” notes are our own analysis.
